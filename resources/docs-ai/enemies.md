# Enemy Catalog & Design Reference

This document is the **single source of truth for enemy design**: every enemy type currently in the game, its stats/behavior, how it's spawned from Tiled, and how to add a new enemy type. It complements — but does not replace — `resources/docs-ai/ashley-ecs.md` (ECS shape: `EnemyComponent`/`EnemySystem`) and `resources/docs-ai/gameplay.md` §2.G (patrol movement logic); this file is where the actual *catalog* of enemy types and their tuning lives.

> **Maintenance rule:** Any time an enemy type is added, removed, renamed, or has its stats/sprite/behavior changed, this file MUST be updated in the same change (see `AGENTS.md` "Enemy Documentation Sync"). If the change also alters `EnemyComponent`/`EnemySystem` shape or introduces a new AI behavior, `ashley-ecs.md` and `gameplay.md` must be updated too, per their own sync rules.

## 1. How enemies are built

*   **Data:** `EnemyComponent` (`core/.../ecs/components/EnemyComponent.java`) — `float health`, `float maxHealth` (starting/full health, set alongside `health`; sizes the death coin drop), `float speed`, `int direction` (`1` right / `-1` left), `float patrolRange` (default `64`), `float originX`, `AiMode aiMode` (`PATROL` default / `SIDE_TO_SIDE`, from the Tiled `aiMode` property), `Timer turnPause` (brief stand-still after each turn-around), `Timer hitStun` (post-hit grace period), `Timer postHitIdle` (brief pause after stun), `boolean isDead`, `Timer deathTimer` (death animation duration + the `0.8s` blink window; removal happens once it elapses), `boolean deathCoinsSpawned` (one-shot guard for the immediate coin drop), `int roomIndex` (the Room index this enemy spawns inside; `-1` = always active), `boolean wasFrozen` (set while the enemy's room is inactive, so the just-zeroed velocity isn't misread as a wall block on the first active frame). Non-shooter enemies also get `EnemyAttackComponent` (`core/.../ecs/components/EnemyAttackComponent.java`) — `AttackType attackType` (`MELEE`), `float attackInterval` (default `2.0s`), `Timer attackCooldown`, `float windUpDuration` (default `0.4s`), `Timer windUp`, `boolean isAttacking`, `float meleeRange` (default `24` world units before `unitScale`), `float attackDamage` (default `1`) — resolved by `EnemyAttackSystem` for melee attacks.
*   **Spawn desync:** Every enemy's patrol cycle is desynchronized at spawn so enemies in the same room don't move/pause in lockstep (they all otherwise spawn with identical state on the same frame): the initial `direction` is randomized (50/50), the patrol `speed` is jittered ±15% (so each enemy drifts out of phase over time and never re-syncs), a flyer's `bobTime` starts at a random phase, a shooter's first shot is staggered across its interval, and an attacker's first attack cooldown is staggered across its interval.
*   **Logic:** `EnemySystem` (`core/.../ecs/systems/EnemySystem.java`) — every frame checks `isDead`: on the first dead frame it spawns the death coin drop immediately (see "Drops on death" below), then ticks `deathTimer` and removes the entity once it elapses (i.e. after the death animation + the ~10Hz `0.8s` blink driven by `AnimationSystem`); otherwise ticks `hitStun` and `postHitIdle`. If `hitStun` just ended, `postHitIdle` is started (0.5s). While either is active, patrol AI is skipped; during `postHitIdle`, velocity is also zeroed so the enemy stays stationary in `IDLE` state before resuming its patrol. Runs at priority `4`, before `MovementSystem` (`5`). When the enemy's room is inactive, it is frozen (velocity zeroed, AI skipped) with `wasFrozen` set; on the first active frame after an unfreeze the wall-block check is skipped once (mirroring the post-turn-pause skip), so re-entering a room doesn't flip every unfrozen enemy in unison.
*   **Physics:** Enemies are ordinary `MovementSystem` family members (Transform+Movement+Collision, non-bullet), so gravity, ground snapping, and AABB wall collision apply automatically — `EnemySystem` only ever touches horizontal (and, during knockback, vertical) velocity.
*   **Damage taken:** Applied externally via the shared `EnemyDamageResolver.applyHit(...)` helper, called from both `CollisionSystem` (bullet hits, `10` damage) and `MeleeAttackSystem` (melee strikes, `5` damage). If `enemy.isDead` or `enemy.hitStun` is active, the hit is ignored entirely; otherwise damage is subtracted. If health reaches 0, the enemy enters its death sequence (zeroed velocity, `DEATH` animation, then a `0.8s` ~10Hz blink before removal after `deathTimer`); otherwise the enemy gets a knockback pop (`90` u/s horizontal away from the attacker, `140` u/s vertical hop) plus a fresh `hitStun` grace period (matching `HURT` animation duration, min `0.3s`) — see "Hit reaction: stun + knockback" below.
*   **Drops on death:** On the first frame `EnemySystem` observes the death — i.e. **immediately on the kill**, before the corpse's linger/blink — it spawns a coin burst at the enemy's collision center via `EntityFactory.popCoins(...)` (guarded by `deathCoinsSpawned` so it fires exactly once): one coin per full `5` points of `EnemyComponent.maxHealth` (`floor(maxHealth / 5)`): `2` for a `10` HP walker/shooter, `1` for the `5` HP flyer, `3` for the `15` HP knight. The coins use the exact same random-velocity pop as chest drops (`80`-`140` u/s up, `±40` u/s horizontal), so every kill yields a small scattershot coin reward (see `gameplay.md` §2.G.10). Like all coins, dropped coins are animated `Coin_01..06` atlas entities (never Tiled tile sprites).
*   **Damage dealt (contact):** `EnemyContactSystem` resolves the reverse direction — on AABB overlap with the player, while `player.hitInvulnerability` (a `com.axehigh.platformer.util.Timer`) is done, it decrements `player.health` by `1` and starts a `1.0s` grace period, so touching an enemy costs one life at most once per second regardless of how many enemies are overlapping.
*   **Damage dealt (melee attack):** Non-shooter enemies (walkers, flyers, knights) can perform a melee attack when the player is within `meleeRange × 3 × unitScale` horizontal distance and `collisionHeight × 2` vertical distance (resolved by `EnemyAttackSystem`). The attack has a wind-up telegraph (`windUpDuration`, default `0.4s`) during which the `ATTACKING` animation plays and the enemy faces the player with velocity zeroed. When the wind-up finishes, a melee hitbox in front of the enemy is checked against the player's AABB; on overlap, `PlayerDamageResolver.applyHit(...)` applies `1` damage + knockback + the shared `2.0s` invulnerability grace period. The attack cooldown (`attackInterval`, default `2.0s`) prevents rapid-fire; staggered at spawn via a random offset. Shooter enemies do NOT get melee attacks by default. Configurable via Tiled properties: `attackType`, `attackInterval`, `meleeRange`, `windUpDuration` — see `gameplay.md` §2.M.
*   **Damage dealt (shots):** A shooter enemy (`EnemyShooterComponent`) additionally fires a bullet every `shootInterval` seconds via `EnemyShootSystem`, but only while the player's active room is the shooter's own room (see "Shooting behavior" below); on hitting the player, `EnemyBulletCollisionSystem` applies the exact same `1` life + `1.0s` grace period rule as contact damage, sharing the **same** `player.hitInvulnerability` timer.
*   **Spawning:** `EnemyFactory.createEnemy(x, y, enemyType, ...)` builds the entity (`TransformComponent`, `TextureComponent`, `AnimationComponent` from `origin-game.atlas`, `MovementComponent`, `CollisionComponent`); the `"enemy"` case in `EntityFactory.spawnObjects` resolves the `enemyType` string to an `EnemyType` enum constant and delegates to it. Placed on the map as a Tiled object-layer marker with `type="enemy"` (see `assets/maps/demo_room.tmx`), usually in a dedicated `"enemies"` layer (though the general `"objects"` layer is also supported); an optional `enemyType` custom string property (`"flyer"` / `"shooter"` / `"knight"`, default `"walker"`) picks the animation prefix (`goblin`, `mosquito`, `spider`, `knight`), stats, and behavior variant — see §3. Per-marker custom properties may additionally override behavior/tuning: `aiMode` (`"side-to-side"`/`"sidetoside"`, case-insensitive) switches the patrol mode (see §2), while `speed`/`patrolRange` override the patrol speed/range in world units — see `map-design-for-tiled.md` §5. On top of any overrides, the spawn desync described in §1 applies: random initial direction, ±15% speed jitter, random flyer bob phase, and staggered first shot for shooters.
*   **Collision Boxes:** Enemies use a reduced AABB bounding box (stored in `CollisionComponent`) that is smaller than their visual 128x128 sprite frame to improve movement feel. These dimensions are defined in `GameConstants.java` (e.g. `EnemyWalkerCollisionWidth/Height`) and scaled by `EnemyWalkerScale`. The box is centered horizontally within the sprite, with a vertical `currentOffsetY` used to align the box with the enemy's feet or visual center.

### Hit reaction: stun + knockback
When a melee or bullet hit lands on an enemy that **isn't** already stunned, `EnemyDamageResolver` applies the damage and, if the enemy survives, gives it a brief "pop" — a `90` u/s horizontal kick away from the attacker (melee: opposite `player.facingDirection`; bullet: the bullet's own travel direction) plus, unless the enemy has a `FlyingEnemyComponent`, a `140` u/s upward hop — then starts a `hitStun` timer matching the `HURT` animation duration (min `0.3s`) and spawns a one-shot spark burst at the enemy's center (`GlobalParticles.SPARKS`, scale `1.2`, hard `1.0s` max lifetime so it doesn't linger). While `hitStun` is active: `EnemySystem`/`EnemyShootSystem` skip patrol/firing (so the knockback velocity isn't immediately overwritten) and the enemy is fully immune to further damage/knockback from either weapon. Once `hitStun` expires, the enemy enters a `0.5s` `postHitIdle` phase where it stays stationary in `IDLE` state before resuming patrol.

### Platform-edge & hazard awareness
Enemies no longer rely solely on the manually-tuned `patrolRange` to avoid falling off a platform: `EnemySystem` also runs a small **ground-sensor probe** every frame — a tiny rectangle positioned just past the enemy's leading foot (in its current travel direction) and just below its feet, checked against the same static `collisionRects` set `MovementSystem` uses for wall/floor collision. If that probe finds no solid ground, the enemy turns around immediately, before it steps off the edge. This works for platforms of any width/shape without per-object tuning. In addition, every grounded enemy refuses to walk over dangerous terrain: a **hazard probe** (a larger rectangle from the enemy's feet up, just past its leading foot) is checked against the map's `hazardRects` (spikes/lava); walking toward a hazard flips the enemy around before it steps in. `patrolRange`/`originX` still apply on top of this as an optional secondary cap (e.g. to keep an enemy patrolling only part of a long platform) — the mechanisms are independent and whichever triggers first wins. Every turn-around (wall, ledge, or hazard) starts a short **turn pause** (`~0.3s`) during which the enemy stands still (`velocity.x` zeroed) so direction changes are visible. Note: these probes are gated on `movement.grounded`, which a flying enemy never is, so they have no effect on flyers (see below).

### AI patrol modes (`AiMode`)
`EnemyComponent.aiMode` selects the patrol behavior — `PATROL` (default) or `SIDE_TO_SIDE`, chosen per-marker via the Tiled `aiMode` property (any other/absent value → `PATROL`):
*   **`PATROL`** — the classic origin-bounded walk: the enemy patrols `patrolRange` units either side of its spawn X (`originX`), turning around at the range limit *and* at walls/ledges/hazards (whichever comes first).
*   **`SIDE_TO_SIDE`** — endless walking: the `originX ± patrolRange` bound is **skipped** entirely, so the enemy walks continuously across a room, turning around only on walls, ledges, and hazards. Intended for ground-walking enemies on wide floors.
A `SIDE_TO_SIDE` flyer still ignores every grounded-only probe, so it flies straight until its `patrolRange` bound turns it (the same constraint as `PATROL` flyers).

### Flying behavior (`FlyingEnemyComponent`, `MovementSystem`, `EnemySystem`)
An enemy tagged with `FlyingEnemyComponent` is skipped entirely by `MovementSystem`'s gravity/wall-slide velocity-Y update, so it never falls under gravity on its own. Instead, every non-stunned frame `EnemySystem` drives a smooth vertical bob wave straight into `movement.velocity.y`, computed from the component's own `bobAmplitude` (`8` world units by default), `bobFrequency` (`π` rad/s, i.e. a ~2 second period), and a running `bobTime` accumulator: `velocity.y = bobAmplitude * bobFrequency * cos(bobTime * bobFrequency)`. Integrated by `MovementSystem` frame over frame, this makes the enemy's height oscillate sinusoidally ±`bobAmplitude` around whatever Y it was at when patrol AI last resumed — a gentle up/down flap while it flies its horizontal patrol path, rather than a perfectly flat line. `bobTime` (and therefore the wave's phase) freezes whenever patrol AI is paused (`hitStun` active), matching the horizontal patrol pause. At spawn `bobTime` is seeded to a random phase (uniform over one full period), so multiple flyers flap out of sync instead of bobbing in unison. Horizontal patrol logic is otherwise untouched: since the entity is never `grounded`, its wall-block, ledge-probe, and hazard-probe turn-around checks never trigger, so it turns around purely on the `originX ± patrolRange` bounds check — meaning a flyer's `patrolRange` **must** be placed clear of walls by the level designer (no automatic wall/edge/hazard avoidance for flyers, regardless of `aiMode`; a `SIDE_TO_SIDE` flyer flies straight until the range bound). On a surviving hit, its knockback pop skips the vertical hop (see "Hit reaction" above) so it never drifts upward permanently; the bob resumes normally once `hitStun` ends.

### Shooting behavior (`EnemyShooterComponent`, `EnemyShootSystem`, `EnemyBulletComponent`)
An enemy tagged with `EnemyShooterComponent` still patrols exactly like a base enemy (it keeps matching `EnemySystem`'s family too). Every frame, `EnemyShootSystem` ticks `shooter.shootCooldown`; while `enemy.hitStun` is active, firing is skipped for that frame (mirrors the patrol stun-pause). Once the cooldown is done, it additionally checks whether the shooter's room is the currently active one — `EnemyComponent.roomIndex == RoomState.activeRoomIndex` (see `gameplay.md` §2.N), the same room tracking `CameraSystem`/`EnemySystem` use; **only if it matches** does it spawn a bullet at the enemy's leading edge traveling horizontally in `enemy.direction` — **no player-aiming/aggro**, purely the enemy's current facing/patrol direction — then restarts the cooldown via `shootInterval` (`5s` by default). At spawn the cooldown is started at a random offset (uniform over one `shootInterval`), so shooters don't all fire on the same frame the player first enters their room. If the player is in a different room, the cooldown is simply left at `isDone()` (never restarted) so the shooter fires as soon as the player re-enters its room, rather than firing blind into a room the player can't currently see. The bullet is tagged with both `BulletComponent` (`damage = 0`, unused) and `EnemyBulletComponent`, which makes `CollisionSystem` ignore it (excluded from its family); `EnemyBulletCollisionSystem` instead resolves its wall/lifetime/player-hit logic, applying the same `1` life + `2.0s` grace period rule as `EnemyContactSystem` (sharing the same timer, so a shot and a touch can't stack damage within the same window).

The table's `Speed` is the base value — each spawned enemy's actual speed is that value jittered ±15% at spawn to desync patrols (see §1).

## 3. Adding a new enemy type

The `enemyType` Tiled custom `string` property on the object marker (read in `EntityFactory.spawnObjects`, default `"walker"` if omitted) is the current per-type discriminator: it resolves to an `EnemyType` enum constant (`map/EnemyType.java`) carrying each type's sprite prefix, walk-clip region name, collision geometry, scale, and max health; `EnemyFactory.createEnemy` then attaches the right marker component (`FlyingEnemyComponent` for `FLYER`, `EnemyShooterComponent` for `SHOOTER`). When introducing another enemy type, follow this pattern and update this table:

1.  **Decide what varies:** stats only (health/speed/patrolRange), sprite only, or new behavior (e.g. chasing the player, a different contact/shot damage amount)?
2.  **Stats/sprite-only variants:** add another constant to the `EnemyType` enum to pick the sprite/stats — no new `Component` needed for this case (this is exactly how the Flying/Shooting Enemy sprites and the flyer's `health = 5f` are wired today).
3.  **New behavior:** if the enemy needs logic `EnemySystem`'s simple patrol can't express (ranged attacks, flying/no-gravity movement, player detection/aggro), prefer a **new marker component** (as done for `FlyingEnemyComponent`/`EnemyShooterComponent`) layered on top of the base `EnemyComponent`, plus either an extension of an existing system (like `MovementSystem`'s `FLYING` gravity opt-out) or a dedicated new `IteratingSystem` (like `EnemyShootSystem`) with a `Family` that includes the new marker. Keep `EnemyComponent` itself (health + patrol fields) as the common base every enemy shares, since damage systems (`CollisionSystem`/`MeleeAttackSystem`) key off it.
4.  **Update docs together:** add the new type's row to the table in §2 here; if `EnemyComponent`/`EnemySystem` (or a new system) changed shape, update `ashley-ecs.md`'s Components/Systems tables and priority list; if the *mechanic* is gameplay-visible, add/extend a subsection under `gameplay.md` §2 alongside the existing "G. Enemy Patrol Movement Logic"/"K. Flying Enemy Movement"/"L. Enemy Shooting & Enemy Bullet Damage".
5.  **Placement:** new enemy types are still placed as Tiled object-layer markers with `type="enemy"`, disambiguated via the `enemyType` property — no change to the map-parsing pipeline itself is required.

## 4. Open design questions (not yet decided)

These are intentionally left unresolved until a concrete need arises — don't design around them speculatively:

*   ~~Whether future enemy types will share `"enemy"` as the Tiled object `type` (disambiguated by a custom property) or get their own `type` value~~ — **resolved:** all four types share `type="enemy"`, disambiguated by the `enemyType` custom property (`"walker"`/`"flyer"`/`"shooter"`/`"knight"`).
*   Whether enemies will ever detect/react to the player (aggro range, chase behavior) — today all three types are non-reactive: the shooter fires in its current facing direction only, with no player-position lookup.
*   ~~Whether enemies will deal contact damage to the player on overlap~~ — **resolved:** `EnemyContactSystem` now applies `1` life of contact damage per enemy touch, gated by `player.hitInvulnerability` (see §1 and `gameplay.md` §2.J). Still open: whether future enemy types will deal *different* amounts of contact/shot damage, or trigger a different grace-period duration — today all three types deal a uniform `1` life per contact/shot.
*   ~~Whether enemies get any hit reaction when damaged~~ — **resolved:** every surviving hit now triggers a knockback pop + `0.3s` stun via `EnemyDamageResolver` (see §1 and `gameplay.md` §2.I), with the vertical hop skipped for flying enemies. Still open: whether future enemy types will use a different knockback strength/stun duration.
*   Whether a stationary-turret variant (shooter that never patrols) will be needed — today the Shooting Enemy always patrols like the walker.
*   ~~Whether per-object Tiled property overrides (custom health/speed/patrolRange/shootInterval per marker, beyond the `enemyType` switch) will be needed, or whether the fixed per-type defaults remain sufficient~~ — **partially resolved:** per-marker `aiMode`, `speed`, and `patrolRange` overrides now exist (see §1 and `map-design-for-tiled.md` §5); a per-marker `health`/`shootInterval` override is still an open option.
